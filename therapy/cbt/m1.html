3. Depression Assessment

Clinical approach

Assess depressive symptoms systematically, including mood, anhedonia, biological symptoms, cognition, functioning and suicidality. Consider screening for bipolarity before conceptualising the presentation as unipolar depression.

5. Childhood Trauma / Abuse Screening

Trauma-informed instruction

Do not require a detailed narrative in Module 1. Establish the nature of exposure, current relevance, safety and symptom domains. Avoid unnecessary graphic details.


"Were there experiences during childhood that made you feel unsafe, frightened, humiliated, neglected or repeatedly hurt?"

6. Explain the CBT Model

Suggested therapist explanation

"CBT looks at the connection between situations, thoughts, emotions, physical reactions and behaviour. We are not going to assume that your thoughts are simply wrong. Instead, we will understand how certain patterns developed and examine whether they are still useful in your current life."


Situation
What happened?
Thought
What went through your mind?
Emotion / Body
What did you feel?
Behaviour
What did you do?
Consequence
What happened next?

Useful therapist responses

Validate: "Given what you experienced, it makes sense that this reaction developed."

Explore: "What makes that interpretation feel convincing?"

Differentiate past from present: "Was that belief useful for surviving then, and does it necessarily describe your situation now?"

Collaborate: "Let's treat this as a hypothesis that we can examine together."
