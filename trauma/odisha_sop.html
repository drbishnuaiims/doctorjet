01

Introduction

⌄

Public perception and opinion regarding a hospital is based on their visit to the accident & emergency department. This facility usually accounts for a substantial number of hospital admissions.

An accident (trauma) or an emergency (casualty) is an injury or illness that is acute and poses an immediate risk to a person's life or long term health.

Casualty and Trauma Care patients often present with potentially life-threatening symptoms such as:

  • Headache
  • Chest pain
  • Abdominal pain
  • Collapse of unknown cause
  • Severe injury, mostly due to road traffic accidents

Trauma patients sustain injury which is unexpected and unannounced, leading to damage, deformity and/or death.

Such patients may reach hospital without prior appointment, either on their own or by ambulance.

Purpose of the SOP

✓ Identify procedures for triage and clear, precise assessment of patients arriving in the Emergency Department.
✓ Improve patient flow from initial reception through discharge or admission.
✓ Ensure patients receive proper care and treatment at the appropriate time.
✓ Enable multidisciplinary teamwork regardless of department workload.
02

Policy

⌄
Mandatory service: All health facilities shall mandatorily provide Casualty and Trauma Care Services.

The manner of disposal of a patient starting from entry into Casualty and Trauma Care until exit from the hospital reflects the alertness and promptness of the hospital.

03

Goal

⌄
The goal is to establish efficient and effective Casualty and Trauma Care services to reduce disability, morbidity and mortality in hospitals.

The SOP emphasizes providing the:

Right treatment
Appropriate treatment for the patient's condition.
Right time
Timely emergency intervention.
Right place
Appropriate area, ward, OT or ICU.
Right resources
Use of available emergency resources.
Science
Evidence-based and appropriate emergency care.
Sympathy & Speed
Compassionate and prompt care.
04

Basic Function of Casualty & Trauma Care Department / Unit

⌄

The Casualty and Trauma Care department/unit shall operate a 24-hour service and provide initial treatment for a broad spectrum of illnesses and injuries that may be life threatening and require immediate attention.

No patient requiring emergency medical care shall be refused adequate treatment even if no bed is available and the particular specialisation is nonexistent.
  1. Provide immediate, appropriate life-saving care and service, efficient and effective, while remaining sensitive to emotional needs and arranging subsequent disposition.
  2. Serve as the definitive specialised care facility, properly equipped and staffed to provide rapid and varied emergency care to people with life-threatening conditions.
  3. Use a triage system of screening and classification to determine priority needs and provide patient care efficiently.
  4. Financial consideration should not be a barrier to initial treatment.
  5. Play a key role during critical interventions of all kinds.
  6. Liaison with courts and police in medico-legal cases.
  7. Public relation information, communication and feedback.
  8. Provide ambulance service.
  9. Education, training and research.

Management of Patients in Triage Area

⌄

Purpose of triage

  • Expedite timely treatment for life-threatening conditions.
  • Ensure appropriate categorisation according to clinical condition.
  • Improve patient flow.
  • Improve patient satisfaction.
  • Decrease overall length of stay.
  • Facilitate streaming of less urgent patients.
  • Be user-friendly for all levels of healthcare professionals.

Initial triage process

On arrival, the patient shall immediately be taken from reception to the waiting/triage area accompanied by an attendant and the registration slip handed to the CMO. This shall be supervised by the Casualty Manager.

The CMO shall conduct a quick examination within 2–4 minutes.

Assessment includes:

✓Life threat
✓Haemorrhage
✓Pain
✓Conscious level
✓Temperature
✓Acuteness

Triage categories and response times

Category Attendance Target Management
IMMEDIATE 0–1 minute Immediately attended and taken to resuscitation bed equipped with oxygen, ventilator and other Cardio Pulmonary Cerebral Resuscitation requirements. After stabilisation, shifted to respective ward.
VERY URGENT Within 15 minutes Immediate intervention.
URGENT Within 30 minutes Prompt emergency assessment and treatment.
LESS URGENT Within 60 minutes Directed to appropriate physician/surgeon for advice and disposal.
NON-URGENT Within 120 minutes Managed in observation bed and discharged after relief or sent to appropriate departmental ward.
DEAD Identification Identification must be made and body shifted to Mortuary until handover to appropriate person or police.
Important: The above time frames apply when patient load is high and discrimination is required for initiation of treatment. Otherwise, all patients shall be attended immediately.

Calls may be escalated at half-hour intervals if the preceding call is not attended. The Superintendent shall subsequently enquire into non-attendance and initiate necessary action if no justified reason is found.
No patient shall remain in the triage area for more than 2 hours.
10

Management of Patients Who Die in Casualty / Trauma Ward

⌄
  1. Death certificate should be issued by the CMO or Senior Resident of the clinical/surgical unit.
  2. CMO should ensure the body is sent to mortuary with due care and promptly inform relatives.
  3. Due courtesy and sympathy should be shown to relatives and assistance provided for disposal of the dead body.
  4. Every casualty death should be reported in writing directly to the Medical Superintendent with particulars and brief resume.
11

Management of Patients Brought Dead to Casualty

⌄
  1. All "brought in dead" cases where actual cause of death is unknown should be handed over to police for suitable action.
  2. Name and available details should be entered in the casualty attendance register, including accompanying relatives' name and address.
  3. Where death occurred due to natural causes and there is no suspicion of foul play, the body may be handed over to relatives on request, with signatures recorded.
  4. Deaths due to accident, assault, burns, suicide, poison, rape or other suspected non-natural causes must be registered as medico-legal cases and police informed.
  5. Out-patient tickets and death reports must be forwarded to Medical Records Section and Registrar Birth & Death.
12

Management of Medico-Legal Cases

⌄
A medico-legal situation is defined as one where there is an allegation, confession or suspicion of causes attributing to body injury or danger to life.

Core CMO principle

The CMO's foremost duty is to render medical aid. The CMO must not enter into arguments with the patient, relatives or attendants regarding medico-legal aspects. The matter should be left to the Police Constable on duty.

MLC documentation requirements

  1. Promptly enter all MLC cases in the medico-legal case register.
  2. Ensure register pages are properly numbered.
  3. Make each entry properly and adequately.
  4. Record name, address, time of arrival, cause and nature of injury clearly.
  5. Record full signature with name of CMO/RS/RP in capital letters.
  6. Enter at least two marks of identification of the patient.
  7. Hand a copy of report and register to police for safe custody.
  8. No unauthorised person should have access to MLC records without written consent of the Medical Superintendent or authorised officer.
  9. Nature of injuries must be recorded in every MLC case.
  10. X-ray reports should be entered within 7 days in the MLC register.
  11. X-ray department is requested to provide X-ray report within 48 hours.
  12. Specialist remarks should be entered and signed by the specialist with name in block letters.
  13. Legal exhibits such as gastric lavage must be immediately sealed and delivered to police with signatures obtained in the book.
  14. For expert medico-legal advice, the faculty on call from Forensic Medicine should be contacted.
  15. MLC reports should be prepared by CMO/RP/RS, not Interns/Junior Residents.
  16. The police officer posted in casualty should expedite completion of MLC reports within 7 days.
13

Preparation of Case Sheet & Management Policy

⌄
The Case Sheet is a legal document. It must be prepared carefully, legibly and contemporaneously.
  1. Case Sheet must be legibly written with full signature, designation, date and time of call/examination.
  2. Haphazard writing with omissions or commissions of facts is strictly to be avoided.
  3. In a grave condition, treatment must start immediately and the Case Sheet maintained subsequently.
  4. Name, age, sex, father's name, full address and accompanying person's name should be clearly written in capital letters.
  5. Provisional diagnosis should be written in capital letters.
  6. Brief and pertinent examination findings should be clearly recorded.
  7. Only investigations essential for diagnosis and immediate management should be advised.
  8. Clear directions should be given for pathology, X-ray, USG, CT and MRI within the hospital.
  9. Patient must be guided to the investigation location; Casualty Manager assistance may be taken.
  10. Direct or indirect indication for investigations through private agencies is strictly condemned and punishable.
  11. No advice should be given to purchase medicines from outside because medicines available in Niramaya are procured according to standard treatment protocol.
  12. Physicians/surgeons should prescribe drugs using generic names, legibly and preferably in capital letters, ensuring rational prescription and use.
  13. Periodic prescription audit should be undertaken.
  14. Erring officers may be held responsible and appropriate action initiated.
  15. Legal action may be taken against staff who mislead patients and cause harassment.
  16. Utmost care should be taken so that patients do not incur expenditure during their stay in Casualty and Trauma Care.
14

Management in Triage & Observation Area

⌄
  • No serious patient needing admission should remain under observation without admission and proper case notes.
  • Staff nurses manage treatment according to physician/surgeon advice.
  • Before taking charge, nurses must check availability of life-saving drugs and oxygen.
  • Any shortage must be indented immediately and kept in stock.
  • Privacy must be provided while dressing private parts.
  • IV drugs and IM drugs to the gluteal region must not be administered in sitting position.
  • IM drugs in the upper limb may be administered while sitting with back support.
  • Injection/drug distribution area must be separate or segregated by partition screen.
  • Aseptic precautions must be taken before parenteral drugs.
  • Dilution and speed of injection administration must follow specification.
  • Expiry date and route of administration must be verified before every injection.
  • Every medicine/injection dispensed must be recorded in a register against the registration number.
  • Drugs, surgical items and medicines must be properly arranged and surroundings kept clean.
  • IV infusion rate and oxygen flow must strictly follow doctor's advice.
  • Administration of IM/IV drugs, wound dressing and nursing care shall be periodically supervised by CMO.
  • Biomedical waste must be appropriately disposed of.
  • Syringes and needles must be destroyed before disposal.
15

Casualty & Trauma Care Admission Policy

⌄
Only patients whose triage assessment falls under Immediate, Very Urgent or Urgent shall be admitted to the trauma ward for further management.

The trauma ward is for emergency care and management and not for continued treatment.

Departmental admission priority

  1. Clinical departments/units should first admit patients already in the trauma ward, followed by OPD patients.
  2. Departmental wards should not keep beds vacant while trauma ward beds are occupied.
  3. No gravely ill patient can be denied attention/admission because of non-availability of beds.
  4. In emergency situations, the CMO may obtain permission of the Medical Superintendent to admit a seriously ill patient in any vacant hospital bed after consultation with RP/RS.

Priority when multiple departments are involved

Clinical Situation Primary Responsibility / Principle
Multiple injuries General Surgery generally takes primary responsibility where abdomen and other systems are involved. Altered sensorium due to head injury is admitted under Neurosurgery.
Surgical + Medical disease The problem of immediate importance decides primary responsibility.
Medicine vs Medical Superspeciality Specific cardiologic, neurologic, nephrologic, endocrinologic or gastroenterological therapeutic measures are managed by concerned superspeciality on advice of Senior Resident.
General Surgery vs Surgical Superspeciality Neurosurgical, cardio-thoracic, surgical gastroenterology, paediatric surgery and plastic surgery requirements are managed by the concerned superspeciality on Senior Resident advice.
Obstetrics/Gynaecology & Psychiatry Same principle of concerned specialty responsibility applies.
The priority decision is indicative. CMO and concerned RP/RS/POC/SOC may jointly decide priority. If unresolved, the Superintendent's decision is final. In no case may treatment be stopped or a patient neglected or deprived of treatment.

Other admission provisions

  • Patients with infectious disease requiring isolation should be admitted to the ID ward.
  • After isolation is no longer required, patient may be shifted to the appropriate ward or discharged.
  • The triage officer/CMO shall direct Less Urgent and Non-Urgent patients to available physician/surgeon for appropriate advice and disposal.
  • There is no provision for back-ward movement of patients in the admission scheme.
16

Management in Trauma Ward

⌄
  • Trauma ward shall be mostly used for accident/surgical patients.
  • It shall be managed on rotation by Surgery and Orthopaedics.
  • Rotation shall be monthly: Surgery one month followed by Orthopaedics.
  • HODs shall prepare day-wise unit distribution.
  • Concerned RS/RP on Call shall be in charge of the trauma ward for the day.
  • He/she shall make frequent rounds and ensure appropriate management and timely shifting.
  • By 9:00 AM daily, concerned POC/SOC shall take all trauma ward cases to respective departmental wards.
  • Post-operative cases operated in Trauma OT may remain in trauma ward until recovery from anaesthesia and stabilisation.
  • If ICU is not required, the stabilised patient should be shifted to the respective ward.
  • CIC/TNO shall ensure shifting.
  • Once admitted to a respective ward or treated in departmental OT, the patient should not again be brought back to trauma ward.

Investigations in Trauma Ward

  • Immediate diagnostic/management investigations only.
  • Pathology/haematology, X-ray and USG in Casualty & Trauma Care.
  • CT and MRI in institutional 24×7 units.
  • No patient should go to private agencies while in trauma ward.
  • Money receipt counter should be in Casualty & Trauma Care.

Medicines and blood

  • Medicines should be procured from Niramaya counter in the Casualty & Trauma Care unit.
  • Medicines unavailable in Niramaya should not be prescribed.
  • Life-saving blood transfusion may be made available up to a maximum of two units.
  • Attendants should be encouraged to donate equal units of blood.

Transfer requirements

  1. Attending doctor must personally evaluate the patient before transfer.
  2. All safety measures and appropriate care must be provided.
  3. Transferring doctor is responsible for appropriate documentation.
  4. Mutual decision with receiving department/unit should be reached.

Discharge / DOR / LAMA

  • Discharging doctor must provide verbal and legibly written follow-up instructions.
  • Discharge certificate must contain patient details, admission date, investigations, diagnosis, treatment and follow-up advice.
  • Full name of treating doctor/unit must be mentioned.
  • Patients refusing further management should be requested to sign the Discharge on Request (DOR) form.
  • DOR form must be attached to the Case Sheet.
  • Refusal to sign should be documented and witnessed.
  • A patient leaving before treatment starts should have this documented, including reason if known, time and signature.
  • Patients leaving against medical advice without hospital knowledge should be marked LAMA in Case Sheet and police intimated.
  • A LAMA register may be maintained.
17

Treatment in OT

⌄
OT must function 24×7.
  • Trauma/emergency patients requiring surgery should be sent to OT from triage.
  • If OT table is unavailable, patient may be kept under observation in trauma ward.
  • After OT, once recovered from anaesthesia and stabilised, patients not requiring ICU should be shifted to respective ward.
  • No stabilised patient should remain in trauma ward unnecessarily.
  • In polytrauma, treatment follows first priority; after stabilisation, patient may move to departmental ward and subsequently to another departmental ward according to priority.
18

Treatment in ICU

⌄
  1. If CMO decides ICU management is required, patient should be immediately shifted to ICU.
  2. Casualty Manager ensures speedy shifting with assistance of one attendant.
  3. After handover to ICU medical officer, Casualty Manager should inform attendants about patient status, line of management and probable outcome and provide brief counselling to reduce anxiety.
  4. Patients from Trauma OT requiring ICU are managed in Trauma ICU.
  5. Once critical condition resolves and patient stabilises, the patient should be shifted to the respective ward.
  6. If required, patient may be kept in departmental or central ICU, preferably not Trauma ICU.
The Trauma ICU must remain ready to accept needy patients from the triage area.
19

Interdepartmental Coordination

⌄
The Casualty & Trauma Care unit is the face of the hospital. Prompt, appropriate and coordinated emergency care increases public confidence in the health-care delivery system.
  • All departments and Casualty & Trauma Care should work as one team.
  • Shifting responsibility from one department to another on technical grounds causing patient suffering is strongly condemned.
  • Every department receiving a referral must examine the patient immediately and provide appropriate advice.
  • In management conflicts such as polytrauma, departments must discuss treatment modalities and decide which intervention should start first.
  • All departments must maintain round-the-clock preparedness.
  • Interdepartmental referral should be made by calling the concerned department and recording the call in the call register.
  • During epidemics and disasters, all appropriate departments share responsibility in a coordinated manner.
  • During such situations, Superintendent is overall supervising authority and ECRO is chief coordinator.
  • All relevant staff must follow directions of Superintendent and ECRO for smooth management.
  • Superintendent's decision is final and binding in such situations.
  • Any call from CMO to RP/RS or POC/SOC should be attended immediately.

Duties & Responsibilities of Staff and Officers

⌄
A. Safai Karmachari
  1. Keep area neat and clean.
  2. Provide clean and disinfected urinals and bedpans.
  3. Carry urine and stool samples to laboratory.
  4. Transport dead bodies to mortuary and dispose of dead foetus/body parts to incinerator.
  5. Clean soiled linen with bleach solution and send to laundry.
  6. Take personal precautions while handling infectious biomaterials.
  7. Be courteous to patients and attendants.
  8. Perform other work assigned by authority.
B. Stretcher Bearer
  1. Remain on duty near casualty entrance or ambulance.
  2. Assist transfer from ambulance to casualty/ward using stretcher, wheelchair or trolley.
  3. Be conversant with first aid treatment.
  4. Perform duties assigned by CMO.
  5. Be polite and sympathetic.
C. Security Personnel
  1. Be polite, tactful, courteous and sympathetic.
  2. Perform duty according to roster.
  3. Regulate patient flow.
  4. Maintain security of area under charge.
  5. Be responsible for untoward incidents in the area.
  6. Perform duties assigned by supervisor/security officer.
D. Attendants
  1. Dusting and disinfection of casualty wards and rooms.
  2. Assist nursing personnel in patient care.
  3. Obtain indents from store and sterilised items from CSSD.
  4. Take referral calls to different departments.
  5. Provide first aid when required.
  6. Assist patient transfers for treatment, investigation and diagnostics.
  7. Give special attention to MLC/critically ill patients until nurse/doctor takes charge.
  8. Assist nursing staff in packing dead bodies for mortuary.
  9. Be courteous and polite.
  10. Perform other assigned duties.
E. Staff Nurses
  1. Attend patients with sincerity and devotion.
  2. Make beds, feed patients, administer injections, infusions and medicines and arrange investigations with Casualty Manager assistance.
  3. Administer oxygen, catheterisation, dressing and toilet.
  4. Maintain pulse, BP and medication/injection records with date and time.
  5. Be polite and sympathetic.
F. Casualty Manager
  1. Under administrative control of Superintendent and directly works under CMO.
  2. Responsible for patient records including admission, management and inter-facility transfer, with record clerk support.
  3. Coordinates registration, movement to triage, resuscitation, ICU, Trauma OT, trauma ward and departmental wards.
  4. Facilitates patient transfer by battery car/Government ambulance and records movement.
  5. Maintains records of deaths brought by 108 ambulance services.
  6. Acts for proper treatment of unknown/destitute and burn patients.
  7. Coordinates mass casualty/natural calamity situations and religious celebrations such as Diwali and Holi.
  8. Coordinates nurses, attendants and vehicle staff.
  9. Supervises sanitation and coordinates Electricity/PHD/Security/Police.
  10. Coordinates RSBY/BKKY/OSTF facilities.
  11. Assists patients regarding Niramaya medicines and investigations.
  12. Performs other duties assigned by authority.
G. Junior Residents (JR) / PG Students
  1. Perform casualty duties on rotation.
  2. Carry out treatment advised by RP/RS or POC/SOC.
  3. Write case sheets neatly and legibly without omissions and in accordance with MCI guidelines.
  4. Put full signature with time of writing; senior doctor must countersign with full signature, date and time.
  5. JR must not sign on behalf of a senior doctor.
  6. Perform minor operative procedures and dressings under Senior Resident guidance.
  7. Make rounds with Senior Resident/RP/RS/POC/SOC.
  8. Handle relatives/attendants tactfully during death.
H. Casualty Medical Officer (CMO)
  1. Remain in casualty with high preparedness and attend patients immediately.
  2. No patient should be denied treatment on the ground that it is not an emergency. Counselling may follow treatment.
  3. Understand physical and psychological condition of patients and attendants.
  4. For extreme unruliness, inform security/police and immediately inform CIC/TNO and Superintendent.
  5. Reach casualty at least 10 minutes before shift to review status and facilitate timely relief.
  6. Ensure life-saving drugs and oxygen are available before shift.
  7. Manage triage without delay and ask attendants to wait in waiting area.
  8. CMO is the final authority in casualty and responsible for complete patient management.
  9. CMO should act as treating doctor and not merely as a referral clerk.
  10. Calls to RS/RP/SOC/POC must be made instantly by phone and recorded in call register.
  11. Actual attending time must be recorded.
  12. No one may sign the call register on behalf of RS/RP/SOC/POC.
  13. Patients should not be sent to wards or OT without being seen/advised by concerned Senior Resident/faculty.
  14. In emergencies requiring a bed, CMO may admit directly to vacant bed after consultation with RP/RS and Superintendent.
I. RP/RS on Duty — Senior Residents
  1. Examine all patients and provide prompt treatment.
  2. Serious patients require consultation with RP/RS on Call.
  3. Transfer patients to ward/OT/ICU on advice of RP/RS on Call.
  4. Cooperate with CMO in MLC documentation.
  5. Ensure history, examination, investigations and provisional diagnosis are documented.
  6. Countersign Case Sheet with full signature, date and time.
  7. Certify death of patients.
  8. Be courteous and polite.
J. RP/POC on Call & RS/SOC on Call
  1. Faculty and Senior Residents on call must inform CMO of whereabouts and mobile numbers.
  2. Emergency calls must be attended immediately and should not wait for completion of OPD/ward rounds.
  3. They should be available in duty rooms during the night.
  4. Departmental call register shall be maintained and staff nurse shall be in charge.
  5. Telephone calls must be recorded with call time.
  6. If admission is required, it should be done immediately.
  7. No admission may be deferred on the plea that no bed is vacant.
  8. Immediate surgical interventions should be performed in Trauma OT.
  9. Guide JRs in history taking and examination of MLC cases.
  10. Train junior doctors in emergency management.
  11. Undue delay is subject to review by CIC/TNO/Superintendent and appropriate action if unjustified.
K. Casualty In-Charge (CIC) / Emergency Control Room Officer (ECRO)
  1. Visit casualty every day by 9:00 AM.
  2. Prepare duty chart of all staff.
  3. Countersign attendance and casualty records.
  4. Forward staff leave applications after arranging duty coverage.
  5. Assess daily readiness.
  6. Ensure cleanliness, sanitation, lighting and ventilation.
  7. Ensure drug and life-saving drug/oxygen indents.
  8. Ensure life-saving drugs, equipment, oxygen and Ambu Bag availability and functionality.
  9. Examine patients when required.
  10. Conduct periodic disaster/mass casualty drills.
  11. Organise regular training involving all departments.
L. Trauma Care Nodal Officer (TNO)
  1. Visit Trauma Ward/Trauma OT/Trauma ICU daily by 9:00 AM.
  2. Prepare duty chart.
  3. Countersign attendance and records.
  4. Forward leave applications after duty arrangements.
  5. Assess readiness of Trauma Ward/OT/ICU.
  6. Ensure cleanliness, sanitation, lighting and ventilation.
  7. Ensure life-saving drugs, equipment, oxygen and Ambu Bag availability.
  8. Examine patients when required.
  9. Conduct disaster/mass casualty drills.
  10. Organise regular training programmes involving all departments.
M. Medical Superintendent
  1. Highest supervising authority.
  2. Visit casualty at least twice weekly.
  3. Ensure patient management follows the SOP.
  4. Review call register and time lag in attending patients.
  5. Examine unjustified delays and initiate appropriate action.
  6. No staff on duty should take leave without duty arrangement and official approval; otherwise absence is unauthorised.
  7. Mobile number, alternate mobile number and residential address of staff should be available in Casualty & Trauma Care.
  8. Periodically assess functioning and suggest modifications.
  9. Ensure deployed staff have no direct or indirect private clinical establishment linkage.
  10. Obtain undertaking regarding absence of such linkage before deployment.
  11. Staff diverting patients to private nursing homes/diagnostic centres may be removed from Casualty & Trauma Care and appropriate action initiated.

Mass casualty responsibilities

  • Mobilise adequate manpower.
  • Ensure adequate life-saving medicines and other requirements.
  • Establish special enquiry/help desk.
  • Provide half-hourly patient status updates.
  • Control crowd.
  • Deploy security and volunteers.
  • Inform police for law-and-order situations.
  • Maintain traffic movement.
  • Ensure smooth ambulance entry and patient disposal.

Ethics of Casualty & Trauma Care

⌄
All citizens of India have a right to emergency medical care.

To fulfil this right, emergency care providers shall:

✓ Abide by institutional and/or Government guidelines on patient management.
✓ Respond promptly and expertly, without prejudice or partiality, to the need for emergency medical care.
✓ Respect patient rights and protect their best interests, particularly vulnerable patients and those unable to make treatment choices due to diminished decision-making capacity.
✓ Communicate truthfully with patients/attendants and obtain informed consent unless urgency requires immediate response.
✓ Manage patients considering psychological, social and financial conditions.
✓ Respect privacy and disclose confidential information only with consent or when required by overriding duty/law.
✓ Deal fairly and honestly with colleagues and protect patients from impaired/incompetent providers or fraud/deception.
✓ Work cooperatively with other stakeholders in emergency care.
✓ Engage in continuing study to maintain knowledge and skills required for high-quality emergency care.
✓ Act as responsible stewards of healthcare resources.
✓ Support societal efforts to improve public health and safety, reduce injury and illness and secure access to emergency and basic healthcare.
